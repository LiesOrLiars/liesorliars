Case File: 007 Organised Crime

The Brooklyn Rabbi's Kidney Ring

◆Part 1: The Market

Every day in America roughly 13 people die waiting for a kidney. Not from a lack of medicine. Not from a lack of surgeons. From a lack of supply. The waiting list for a kidney in New York runs 7 to 9 years. Nationally over 90,000 people sit and wait.

Kidneys are the most trafficked organ in the world because they can be taken from living donors. One person walks in with two. One gets removed. The seller walks out alive. The buyer gets a future. Somebody in the middle collects the difference.

That somebody, for roughly a decade, was Levy Izhak Rosenbaum of Borough Park, Brooklyn. He told his neighbours he was in construction.

◆Part 2: The Man

Rosenbaum was an Israeli citizen living legally in the United States. A member of the Orthodox Jewish community in Borough Park, Brooklyn. Heavyset. Unremarkable. Respected enough that nobody asked questions.

He was not a rabbi. Not a doctor. Not licensed in any medical capacity at all. None of that mattered.

He began brokering kidney sales around 1999. For the next decade he operated in plain sight. He arranged transplants. He flew donors in from Israel. He coached them on what to say. He spread money to the right people and kept his name off everything that mattered.

Researchers who tracked him called him the top man in the United States. He called himself a matchmaker.

◆Part 3: The Network

The operation ran deeper than one man in Brooklyn. Rosenbaum worked through an Israeli boss who organized the Israeli patients and matched them to strangers who would pose as altruistic donors. He employed kidney hunters intermediaries paid by the head to troll immigration offices, jails, unemployment offices, factories, and public housing. Looking for the debtors. The displaced. The desperate.

Many of the donors were desperately poor immigrants from Moldova, Romania and Russia. People who needed cash fast enough that surgery on a stranger's terms seemed like a reasonable trade.

Once a donor was found the machinery moved. Blood samples collected. Paperwork prepared. A story constructed to tell the hospital. Flights booked. Safe houses organized in Brooklyn. There was a minder to make sure the sellers would not flee.

The donors received $10,000. Sometimes less. The recipients paid $120,000 to $160,000. Rosenbaum kept the spread.

◆Part 4: The Coercion

The network called the donors willing. The record told a different story.

When a potential organ seller would get cold feet, Rosenbaum would use his finger to simulate firing a gun at the person's head. He carried a real one too, according to researchers who tracked his operation. One former associate described the message plainly: "You are here. There is no leaving."

Donors were brought to a country where they did not speak the language. Held in a house they did not control. Their return flights often tied to compliance.

Elahn Quick sold his kidney for $25,000 in 2008. As he lay on the operating table having second thoughts his caretaker held his hand and told him it was not too late. Before he could finish the conversation he lost consciousness. He woke up hours later after the surgery. The caretaker was gone.

◆Part 5: The Tape

The meeting that brought Rosenbaum down took place in his basement in February 2008. An undercover FBI agent came in posing as someone whose uncle was dying of kidney failure. Rosenbaum invited them into his home.

He explained the process. Blood samples would need to be taken. Sent to Israel for compatibility testing. A cover story would need to be created explaining the relationship between recipient and donor.

"It is really easy to find out if he is not family," he said on tape. "It is our job to prepare him."

He boasted openly: "So far I have never had a failure. I am doing this a long time."

The price was $160,000. The uncle did not exist. The agent kept recording.

◆Part 6: The Arrest and Sentence

On July 23, 2009 the FBI moved. 44 people arrested in a single morning 3 mayors, 5 Orthodox rabbis, 2 state legislators, and one kidney broker.

It was the first federal charge of organ trafficking in American history.

In October 2011 Rosenbaum pleaded guilty. 3 counts related to kidney brokering and 1 count of conspiracy. He earned $410,000 from 3 confirmed black market sales.

His lawyers argued he had saved lives. The recipients were real. The kidneys worked.

Rosenbaum spoke before sentencing: "It was wrong but I thought the cause was good."

Judge Anne Thompson handed down the sentence in July 2012: 2 and a half years.

He was released in late 2014 and returned to Brooklyn. He is still there. The waiting list is longer now than it was in 2009. The gap is wider. The market did not close.
