Case File: 029 Government & Corruption

The Judge Never Told Them About The Money

Published 15 November 2026

How this file was sourced: official records only, listed in Sources below.

◆Part 1: The Buttons

A 16 year old girl stood before Luzerne County juvenile judge Mark Ciavarella. She had driven the wrong way down a one way street without a licence. It was the first time she had been in trouble.

She testified in federal court in 2021. She said Ciavarella told her to count the buttons on her blouse. That was the number of months she was going away for. She counted them. There were 11.

She spent 11 months in detention.

Ciavarella later admitted that from 2003 to 2007 he never told any juvenile who appeared before him that he was receiving money from PA Child Care, Western PA Child Care or Robert Powell, a part owner of both.

Federal court records describe more than 2.8 million dollars paid to Ciavarella and President Judge Michael Conahan by Powell and by Robert Mericle, the builder of both centres.

In 2009 the Pennsylvania Supreme Court ordered every adjudication of delinquency and consent decree Ciavarella entered between 1 January 2003 and 31 May 2008 vacated. A state commission expected the order to cover about 4,000 cases.

◆Part 2: The Facility

Ciavarella sat on the Luzerne County Court of Common Pleas in Pennsylvania from 1996 to January 2009. He served mainly in juvenile court. Conahan was President Judge from January 2002 to the end of 2006. Ciavarella succeeded him in January 2007.

In 2000 the judges and county officials raised concerns about the condition of the county juvenile detention facility. Some officials wanted a new county run centre. Ciavarella pushed for a private facility that would be leased to the county.

The Court of Appeals recorded that the 2 judges helped bring the investors together. Powell, a lawyer and friend of Conahan, and a business partner created PA Child Care LLC to develop the centre. He hired Mericle Construction to build it. Its owner, Mericle, was a friend of Ciavarella.

In July 2001 Mericle told Ciavarella he would pay him a referral fee of 10 percent of the contract price. Ciavarella asked him to route it through Powell. The judges agreed to split it because Conahan had put the deal together.

Ciavarella described the old facility under oath in July 2009: this place is a dump and it shouldn't be open.

◆Part 3: The Lease

In late 2001 Powell had trouble getting a bank loan. The judges agreed to create a lease between PA Child Care and the county to secure it.

Conahan signed the lease in January 2002, after he became President Judge and had authority to bind the county. The Court of Appeals recorded that the judges kept county officials from seeing it.

It committed the county to pay PA Child Care 1,314,000 dollars a year. The agreement stated that the obligation to pay was absolute and unconditional.

The federal charges stated that Conahan, as President Judge, removed funding for the county owned detention facility from the county budget, and it closed. A federal court later recorded that in December 2002 both judges appeared on television and spoke about the need to shut down existing juvenile facilities.

In January 2002 Conahan appointed Ciavarella to the juvenile court. In 2004 PA Child Care was awarded a county contract worth about 58 million dollars.

◆Part 4: The Finder's Fee

In February 2002 Mericle and PA Child Care signed a construction contract. It included a referral fee of 997,600 dollars, payable when the building was finished.

In January 2003 Conahan gave Powell wire transfer instructions. The money moved through a series of people and companies so that it could not be traced. Mericle Construction reported the payment for tax. Ciavarella never reported the income.

Ciavarella testified in July 2009 that the money went from Mericle's company to a man named Robert Matta, then to a firm owned by Conahan called Beverage Marketing, and then to him. He put his share at 440,000 dollars.

He described telling Conahan about the fee. He said Conahan replied: that is one hell of a friend you have.

Powell and Mericle built a second centre, Western PA Child Care, and expanded the first from 48 beds to 60. In July 2005 Mericle paid a 1 million dollar fee for the second centre. In February 2006 he paid 150,000 dollars for the expansion.

For the February 2006 payment Mericle followed Ciavarella's instructions and transferred the funds to the Pinnacle Group of Jupiter LLC, a company the judges and their wives had formed in January 2004. In all, Mericle paid 2,147,600 dollars in referral fees to Ciavarella and Conahan.

◆Part 5: The Rent

PA Child Care opened by February 2003. Ciavarella kept regular track of how many of its beds were in use. In 2003 alone he detained more than 100 juveniles there.

In November 2003 the judges called Powell to a meeting about how many juveniles Ciavarella had sent and what the county had paid. The Court of Appeals recorded that Ciavarella concluded the centre was doing very, very well and that he wanted a part of it.

In February 2004 Pinnacle bought an uninhabitable condominium in Jupiter, Florida. From January to September 2004 Powell sent Pinnacle 590,000 dollars in cheques marked as rent and marina fees.

In June 2006 the judges called Powell to another meeting. Powell testified that they told him: Look, you're in this business, we helped you get into it, you're making a lot of money, you're going to give us some.

He testified that he was not paying them for any services. He was paying them because they demanded it in their position of authority.

From August to December 2006 Powell paid 143,500 dollars in cash. It was delivered in boxes to Conahan and his judicial aide.

Ciavarella gave his own account under oath in July 2009. He said the payments from Powell were rent on the Florida condominium at 10,000 dollars a month, prepaid for 6 years. He said Powell only ever paid 520,000 dollars.

◆Part 6: The Courtroom

In 2001 the Pennsylvania Superior Court reversed Ciavarella for placing a 13 year old in detention without informing him of his right to counsel. The state commission recorded that a newspaper then quoted him: I'll never do it again.

From 1 October 2005 a court rule required judges to question any juvenile who waived counsel on the record, to make sure the waiver was understood.

A special master appointed by the Pennsylvania Supreme Court found 1,866 cases in which juveniles appeared before Ciavarella without counsel between 2003 and 2008. He concluded that none of them had knowingly and intelligently waived that right. He concluded that Ciavarella knew he was violating the law and court rules.

In 54 percent of the juvenile cases Ciavarella decided between 2003 and 2008, the juvenile had no lawyer.

In 2003 he ordered 330 juveniles into placement. That was twice the statewide average. Luzerne County held less than 3 percent of the state population. Ciavarella accounted for 22 percent of all juvenile placements in Pennsylvania.

The Court of Appeals recorded that in many cases he disregarded the recommendations of juvenile probation officers and ordered detention. On some occasions officers changed their recommendations from release to detention under pressure from him.

In 2021 a federal court heard from 282 former juveniles and 32 parents. 79 of the former juveniles had been under 13. A boy of 15 testified that Ciavarella told him he did not need a lawyer. He said the hearing lasted 42 seconds and he was locked up for about 10 months.

A girl of 14, cited for underage drinking, testified that Ciavarella told her juvenile delinquents do not get to hug their parents goodbye. A girl of 10 testified that he told her there was no such thing as self defence. Others were detained for stealing a Hershey bar, writing on a school window and being a few minutes late for curfew.

◆Part 7: The Wire

In 2007 the judges and Powell learned they were under federal investigation when witnesses received grand jury subpoenas.

In November 2007 Ciavarella met Mericle. The Court of Appeals recorded that he told Mericle he could go to jail if Mericle reported paying him a referral fee through Powell. He encouraged Mericle to destroy records.

In January 2008 the judges met Powell to coordinate their stories. They told him to testify that he had never given them any boxes of cash.

By the summer of 2008 Powell was cooperating with investigators. He wore a recording device in his conversations with the judges. In July 2008 the 3 men discussed how to discredit Powell's law partner if she reported delivering his cash to Conahan.

On 29 April 2008 the Juvenile Law Center asked the Pennsylvania Supreme Court to stop Luzerne County holding delinquency hearings without counsel. In May 2008 Ciavarella removed himself from juvenile court. On 8 January 2009 the Supreme Court declined to intervene.

◆Part 8: The Charges

On 26 January 2009, 18 days later, the United States Attorney filed criminal charges against both judges. They were accused of taking 2.6 million dollars in payoffs. Each signed a plea agreement for 87 months in prison. On 28 January 2009 the Pennsylvania Supreme Court removed both judges from judicial service.

Powell was charged on 9 June 2009 and pleaded guilty on 1 July. He agreed to forfeit a 56 foot yacht named Reel Justice and a corporate jet. At his plea hearing he said there was no quid pro quo per se. Mericle was charged in August 2009 with misprision of a felony, for concealing his knowledge of the judges' tax conspiracy, and pleaded guilty. As part of his plea agreement he contributed 2,150,000 dollars to fund programmes for the children of Luzerne County.

On 2 July 2009 Ciavarella testified in an unrelated civil case and described the payments. He said: I did not consider what I did to be illegal. He acknowledged that he had not paid taxes on the money.

In July 2009 Judge Edwin Kosik rejected both plea agreements. He wrote that Ciavarella continues to deny what he terms 'quid pro quo' his receipt of money as a finder's fee. He wrote that the presentence report recorded Conahan refusing to discuss the motivation behind his conduct.

In September 2009 a grand jury returned a 48 count indictment. On 29 April 2010 Conahan agreed to plead guilty to racketeering conspiracy.

Ciavarella went to trial in February 2011. He testified that the payments from Mericle were finder's fees, honest money with no connection to his work as a judge. He denied receiving any payment from Powell. His defence argued there was no agreement to send juveniles to the centres in exchange for money.

On 18 February 2011 the jury convicted him on 12 of 39 counts, including racketeering, honest services mail fraud, money laundering conspiracy and filing false tax returns. It acquitted him of bribery, extortion, money laundering and honest services wire fraud. It found he should forfeit 997,600 dollars.

On 11 August 2011 Kosik sentenced him to 336 months. 28 years. The guidelines called for life. He was ordered to pay 1,173,791.94 dollars in restitution.

On 23 September 2011 Conahan was sentenced to 17 and a half years. On 25 April 2014 Mericle was sentenced to 1 year in prison and fined 250,000 dollars.

◆Part 9: The Record

On 29 October 2009 the Pennsylvania Supreme Court gave its reasons for vacating the cases: this Court cannot have any confidence that Ciavarella decided any Luzerne County juvenile case fairly and impartially. It cited his admission that he received the payments and failed to disclose his financial interests in the facilities.

On 24 May 2013 the Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit affirmed the convictions and the sentence, except for 1 count of honest services mail fraud, which it found was barred by the statute of limitations.

In 2018 the district court vacated the racketeering, racketeering conspiracy and money laundering conspiracy convictions. It found his trial lawyer had been ineffective for failing to raise a statute of limitations defence. The government did not retry him. On 24 August 2020 the court refused to resentence him. The 336 month sentence stood on the remaining counts.

On 16 August 2022 Judge Christopher Conner entered judgment against both former judges for 106,296,945.53 dollars in compensatory damages and 100 million dollars in punitive damages. Ciavarella and Conahan had waived their right to take part in the trial.

Judge Conner recorded that several of the former juveniles who were alive when the lawsuit began had since died from overdoses or suicide. He wrote: A more appropriate case for punitive damages is difficult to conjure.

On 12 December 2024 Conahan was among 1,499 people whose federal sentences were commuted by President Joseph Biden.

Ciavarella kept track of the beds. He never told the children about the money.
