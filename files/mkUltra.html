MK Ultra: The CIA's Mind Control Program

Updated 20 October 2026 to correct the 1996 District Attorney finding and the dates of MK Ultra against Olson v. United States (2013) and the 1977 Senate hearing on Project MKULTRA.

Published 26 August 2026

◆Part 1: The Drugging

November 1953. Deep Creek Lake, Maryland. CIA scientists from the Technical Services Division gathered with Army researchers from Fort Detrick. These were men who built weapons. Biological weapons. Chemical weapons. Weapons designed to break the human mind and body.

After dinner, Sidney Gottlieb made a decision.

Gottlieb ran a CIA program called MK Ultra. The goal was to find ways to control the human mind. That night, Gottlieb ordered his deputy Robert Lashbrook to spike the Cointreau served after dinner with LSD. The men drinking had no idea. No knowledge. No consent.

One man, Frank Olson, a biological warfare scientist from Fort Detrick, was affected more deeply than the others. 9 days later, Frank Olson was dead.

◆Part 2: Who Was Frank Olson

Frank Olson was not some random civilian caught in a CIA experiment. He was a biological warfare expert. His job was developing weapons. Anthrax. Tularemia. Brucellosis. Diseases turned into aerosols.

He talked about quitting. He expressed doubts about the work. He questioned whether what they were doing was right.

In the world of classified Cold War weapons development, doubt was dangerous.

Then came the retreat at Deep Creek Lake. Then came the LSD. Then came 9 days of psychological unraveling.

◆Part 3: The Fall

November 28, 1953. 2:30 in the morning. Hotel Statler, New York City. Thirteenth floor. Room 1018A.

Frank Olson and Robert Lashbrook were staying together. Lashbrook was Gottlieb's deputy, assigned to watch Olson. The CIA was concerned. Olson was unstable. He knew too much.

At 2:30 AM, Olson's body hit the sidewalk outside the hotel. The window in room 1018A was shattered. He had gone through it. Thirteenth floor to pavement. He died minutes later.

Lashbrook made a call. Not to the police. He said: "He's gone." The voice on the other end replied: "Well, that's too bad." Then hung up.

No panic. No shock. Just acknowledgment. As if this outcome had been anticipated.

◆Part 4: The Cover Up

The CIA moved quickly. They told Olson's wife that there had been an accident. They did not mention the LSD. No autopsy was ordered. CIA officials controlled the funeral proceedings.

For 22 years, the official story was: Frank Olson, troubled scientist, killed himself in a New York hotel. The family believed it because they had no reason not to.

Then in 1975, the Rockefeller Commission began investigating CIA abuses. The commission uncovered MK Ultra. The New York Times picked up the story. Suddenly, 22 years after his death, Frank Olson's name was back in the news.

The CIA admitted what had happened. Olson had been dosed with LSD as part of an MK Ultra experiment. President Gerald Ford invited the Olson family to the White House. He apologised on behalf of the government. Congress authorised a payment of $750,000 to the family.

It seemed like closure. But the family was not satisfied. The story still did not make sense.

◆Part 5: MK Ultra Revealed

MK Ultra was not one experiment. It was 149 subprojects. In 1977 the CIA told the Senate the project had run from 1953 to 1964.

The goal was mind control. The methods were unlimited.

LSD given to unwitting subjects: prisoners, mental patients, ordinary citizens dosed without consent. Sensory deprivation. Subjects placed in isolation tanks for days or weeks. Electroshock therapy: unregulated shocks designed to erase memory. Hypnosis combined with drugs. Torture. Sexual humiliation.

Gottlieb funded university researchers who conducted studies on students, prisoners, and psychiatric patients. He set up safe houses where CIA operatives dosed people with LSD and filmed them through one way mirrors.

In January 1973 staff of the Technical Services Division destroyed the MK Ultra records on Gottlieb's verbal orders. Gottlieb testified, and Helms confirmed, that he was carrying out the verbal order of Richard Helms, then Director of Central Intelligence. Records the CIA found later, after a Freedom of Information request, showed 86 universities or institutions had been involved.

◆Part 6: The Exhumation

1994. 41 years after Frank Olson's death, his sons had the body exhumed for a new autopsy.

The autopsy found a hematoma on Olson's temple.

Forensic scientist James Starrs examined the remains. Starrs concluded the injury came from a blow to the head before the fall. Starrs concluded the evidence was inconsistent with suicide and suggested homicide.

The official record gave his death as suicide. Lashbrook, who shared the room, gave inconsistent statements after the death, including on whether he had seen Olson jump.

In 1996 the New York District Attorney's Office reopened its file on the death. After a long investigation it changed the cause of death from suicide to unknown.

◆Part 7: The Scope

One of them, MK Ultra Subproject 68, was conducted by Dr. Ewen Cameron at McGill University in Montreal. Cameron used a technique he called "psychic driving." Patients were put into comas with drugs for weeks. They were played looped audio messages hundreds of times a day. They were given electroshock therapy at levels far beyond standard medical practice.

The goal was to erase existing memories and reprogram the patient with new ones. It did not work. What it did do was destroy people's minds. Patients lost the ability to recognise family members. They forgot how to use a toilet. Some never recovered.

Cameron's work was funded by the CIA through a front organisation. The patients had no idea. They thought they were receiving treatment for depression or anxiety.

How many people were experimented on? How many died? How many suffered permanent harm? We do not know. We will never know. The people who ran the program made sure of that.

◆Part 8: No Accountability

When MK Ultra was exposed in 1975, there was public outrage. Congressional hearings were held. The Church Committee investigated. Witnesses testified. Documents were declassified.

And then nothing happened.

No one was prosecuted. No one was charged. No one went to jail.

Sidney Gottlieb, who ran the programme and gave the order to destroy its records, retired to a farm in Virginia. He raised goats. He died in 1999, a free man.

The researchers who conducted experiments on unwitting subjects retired with pensions. The institutions that hosted the research faced no consequences.

They got away with it.

◆Part 9: The Question

When the full scope of MK Ultra became public, the public asked: how could this happen in America?

The answer is simple. It happened because it was allowed to happen. Congress did not provide oversight. The media did not investigate. The scientific community did not object. The justice system did not prosecute.

Institutions do what they can get away with. MK Ultra happened because the CIA got away with it.

Sidney Gottlieb died a free man. Frank Olson died in 1953. His cause of death is now recorded as unknown. No one was held accountable.

If an institution can drug an American citizen without his knowledge, keep it from his family for 22 years and face no consequences when exposed, what else can that institution do?

The question is not what else are they hiding. The question is: when did we decide this was acceptable?
