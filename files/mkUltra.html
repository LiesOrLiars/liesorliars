MK Ultra: The CIA's Mind Control Program

◆Part 1: The Drugging

November 1953. Deep Creek Lake, Maryland. CIA scientists from the Technical Services Division gathered with Army researchers from Fort Detrick. These were men who built weapons. Biological weapons. Chemical weapons. Weapons designed to break the human mind and body.

After dinner, Sidney Gottlieb made a decision.

Gottlieb ran a CIA program called MK Ultra. The goal was simple: find ways to control the human mind. That night, Gottlieb ordered his deputy Robert Lashbrook to spike the after-dinner Cointreau with LSD. The men drinking had no idea. No knowledge. No consent.

One man, Frank Olson, a biological warfare scientist from Fort Detrick, was affected more deeply than the others. Nine days later, Frank Olson was dead.

◆Part 2: Who Was Frank Olson

Frank Olson was not some random civilian caught in a CIA experiment. He was a biological warfare expert. His job was developing weapons. Anthrax. Tularemia. Brucellosis. Diseases turned into aerosols.

Summer 1953. Olson was sent to Germany. He witnessed what the CIA called "terminal interrogations." People were interrogated using experimental drugs and techniques. Some of those people died during interrogation. That was the point.

Olson saw it happen. In Frankfurt. In Berlin. In Heidelberg. He came back to the United States a different man. He talked about quitting. He expressed doubts about the work. He questioned whether what they were doing was right.

In the world of classified Cold War weapons development, doubt was dangerous.

Then came the retreat at Deep Creek Lake. Then came the LSD. Then came nine days of psychological unraveling.

◆Part 3: The Fall

November 28, 1953. 2:30 in the morning. Hotel Statler, New York City. Thirteenth floor. Room 1018A.

Frank Olson and Robert Lashbrook were staying together. Lashbrook was Gottlieb's deputy, assigned to watch Olson. The CIA was concerned. Olson was unstable. He knew too much.

At 2:30 AM, Olson's body hit the sidewalk outside the hotel. The window in room 1018A was shattered. He had gone through it. Thirteenth floor to pavement. He died minutes later.

Lashbrook made a call. Not to the police. He said: "He's gone." The voice on the other end replied: "Well, that's too bad." Then hung up.

No panic. No shock. Just acknowledgment. As if this outcome had been anticipated.

◆Part 4: The Cover Up

The CIA moved quickly. They told Olson's wife that there had been an accident. They did not mention the LSD. No autopsy was ordered. CIA officials controlled the funeral proceedings.

For 22 years, the official story was: Frank Olson, troubled scientist, killed himself in a New York hotel. The family believed it because they had no reason not to.

Then in 1975, the Rockefeller Commission began investigating CIA abuses. The commission uncovered MK Ultra. The New York Times picked up the story. Suddenly, 22 years after his death, Frank Olson's name was back in the news.

The CIA admitted what had happened. Olson had been dosed with LSD as part of an MK Ultra experiment. President Gerald Ford invited the Olson family to the White House. He apologized on behalf of the government. Congress authorized a payment of $750,000 to the family.

It seemed like closure. But the family was not satisfied. The story still did not make sense.

◆Part 5: MK Ultra Revealed

MK Ultra was not one experiment. It was 149 sub-projects conducted over more than 20 years, starting in 1953 and continuing until 1973, when CIA Director Richard Helms ordered most of the records destroyed.

The goal was mind control. The methods were unlimited.

LSD experiments on unwitting subjects prisoners, mental patients, ordinary citizens dosed without consent. Sensory deprivation. Subjects placed in isolation tanks for days or weeks. Electroshock therapy unregulated shocks designed to erase memory. Hypnosis combined with drugs. Torture. Sexual humiliation.

Gottlieb funded university researchers who conducted studies on students, prisoners, and psychiatric patients. He set up safe houses where CIA operatives dosed people with LSD and filmed them through two-way mirrors.

When the program attracted scrutiny, Gottlieb personally ordered the destruction of MK Ultra records. He retired in 1973. Most of the documentation was shredded. What we know comes from a few thousand documents that survived.

◆Part 6: The Exhumation

1994. Forty-one years after Frank Olson's death. His son Eric pushed to exhume the body and conduct a proper autopsy.

What the forensic experts found changed everything. There was a hematoma on Olson's skull a cranial injury. Not from the fall. From before the fall.

Frank Olson had been struck in the head with a blunt object before he went through the window.

Forensic pathologist James Starrs issued a report: the evidence was inconsistent with suicide. The evidence suggested homicide. Olson had been knocked unconscious, then thrown or pushed through the window.

The window in room 1018A was closed when Olson allegedly jumped. A closed window requires significant force to go through. But Olson's injuries didn't match someone who had taken a running leap.

The New York District Attorney opened an investigation. In 1996, the Manhattan DA's office announced they could not bring charges too much time had passed but the statement was clear: the death was not a suicide. Frank Olson had been murdered.

◆Part 7: The Scope

One sub-project, MK Ultra Subproject 68, was conducted by Dr. Ewen Cameron at McGill University in Montreal. Cameron used a technique he called "psychic driving." Patients were put into drug-induced comas for weeks. They were played looped audio messages hundreds of times a day. They were given electroshock therapy at levels far beyond standard medical practice.

The goal was to erase existing memories and reprogram the patient with new ones. It did not work. What it did do was destroy people's minds. Patients lost the ability to recognize family members. They forgot how to use a toilet. Some never recovered.

Cameron's work was funded by the CIA through a front organization. The patients had no idea. They thought they were receiving treatment for depression or anxiety.

How many people were experimented on? How many died? How many suffered permanent harm? We do not know. We will never know. The people who ran the program made sure of that.

◆Part 8: No Accountability

When MK Ultra was exposed in 1975, there was public outrage. Congressional hearings were held. The Church Committee investigated. Witnesses testified. Documents were declassified.

And then nothing happened.

No one was prosecuted. No one was charged. No one went to jail.

Sidney Gottlieb, who ran the program and ordered the destruction of records, retired to a farm in Virginia. He raised goats. He died in 1999, a free man.

Richard Helms, the CIA director who ordered the destruction of MK Ultra files, was later convicted of perjury for lying to Congress about unrelated CIA activities but received only a suspended sentence and small fine.

The researchers who conducted experiments on unwitting subjects retired with pensions. The institutions that hosted the research faced no consequences.

They got away with it.

◆Part 9: The Question

When the full scope of MK Ultra became public, the public asked: how could this happen in America?

The answer is simple. It happened because it was allowed to happen. Congress did not provide oversight. The media did not investigate. The scientific community did not object. The justice system did not prosecute.

Institutions do what they can get away with. MK Ultra happened because the CIA got away with it.

Sidney Gottlieb died a free man. Richard Helms faced no real consequences. Frank Olson was murdered in 1953. The truth came out in 1994. And still, no one was held accountable.

If an institution can murder an American citizen, cover it up for 40 years, and face no consequences when exposed what else can that institution do?

The question is not what else are they hiding. The question is: when did we decide this was acceptable?
