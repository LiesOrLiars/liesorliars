Case File: 030 Corporate Crime

The Machine Was Running A Null Protocol

Published 18 November 2026

How this file was sourced: official records only, listed in Sources below.

◆Part 1: The Demonstration

The guests were shown into a room with a Theranos device. They were led to believe it was running a sample of their blood.

It was not. Theranos employees testified that the device was running a null protocol. Some of the guests' samples were run in secret on machines made by other companies. The guests included investors. The US Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit set out that testimony in 2025.

Theranos developed 300 small sample assays while Elizabeth Holmes ran the company. The court recorded that only 12 were ever run on its own device, the Edison.

On 18 November 2022 a federal judge sentenced Holmes to 135 months in prison for defrauding Theranos investors. In May 2023 the court held her and Ramesh Balwani, the company's former president, jointly liable for 452,047,268 dollars in restitution.

◆Part 2: The Company

Holmes founded Theranos in 2003. She served as its chief executive officer until 2018. The company was based in Palo Alto and Newark, California.

The Court of Appeals recorded her claimed goal: technology to run blood tests on small samples of blood drawn from a fingerstick, rather than from a vein.

Balwani joined in 2009. He later became President and Chief Operating Officer. The court recorded that he and Holmes were in a romantic relationship from 2004 to 2016. Evidence at both trials showed that he oversaw operations in the patient testing lab and was the main contact for the retail partners Safeway and Walgreens.

Holmes claimed the Theranos analyser could run any blood test run by conventional labs. The US Attorney's Office summarised the claim: results better, cheaper and more accurate than existing methods, at a speed faster than ever before possible.

In 2013 Balwani reviewed and approved a Wall Street Journal article. It said Theranos had devices that automate and miniaturize more than 1,000 laboratory tests.

Holmes admitted that not everyone reported directly to her. She also admitted that ultimately all roads led to her as the CEO.

◆Part 3: The Edison

The Edison was the only device Theranos developed that was ever used to test patient samples. It was also called the 3.5 and the Theranos Proprietary System.

Other general chemistry tests were run on machines made by third parties. Theranos modified some of them. Some it used unmodified.

The Securities and Exchange Commission later charged that the company conducted the vast majority of patient tests on modified and industry standard commercial analysers manufactured by others.

Theranos employees testified at both trials that the Edison consistently failed quality control checks and could not provide accurate results. They told Holmes and Balwani at the time. The Court of Appeals recorded that the 2 executives dismissed the concerns or shifted blame away from the device.

It recorded that they pushed lab employees to move as many tests as possible onto the Edison, despite the employees' concerns about its accuracy.

Multiple investors testified that Holmes and Balwani led them to believe Theranos ran its tests only on devices it made itself. They said the truth would have affected their decisions to invest.

◆Part 4: The Numbers

In 2010 Theranos signed agreements with Walgreens and Safeway to bring blood testing into their stores. In the autumn of 2013 it launched testing at Walgreens. That year Wellness Centers opened in Palo Alto and in Phoenix and Scottsdale, Arizona.

Theranos then raised money by selling new shares to investors in late 2013 and in 2014. The shares were not publicly traded.

The company's corporate controller testified that Theranos had no revenue in 2012 or 2013. It lost 57 million dollars and 92 million dollars in those years. In November 2013 Balwani told Holmes the company was down to 15 million dollars in cash. It was spending up to 2 million dollars a week. In 2015 Theranos reported 1,944,948 dollars in total income to the IRS.

Investors were shown something else. 1 investor testified that documents from Holmes and Balwani projected a profit of 230 million dollars by the end of 2015. Another testified that they told him Theranos had generated 200 million dollars in revenue from the Department of Defense.

Trial evidence showed that Holmes told investors Theranos was projected to generate over 100 million dollars in revenue in 2014 and nearly 1 billion dollars in 2015. The SEC charged that it generated a little more than 100,000 dollars in revenue from operations in 2014.

By March 2015 Theranos had raised more than 730 million dollars from investors in those 2 share rounds.

◆Part 5: The Battlefield And The Logos

Investors testified that they were led to believe the Theranos device was in use by the military. General James Mattis testified at Holmes's trial. He said the Edison was never put into the field by the military, never installed on a military medevac and never used to treat servicemembers on the battlefield.

The jury at Holmes's trial heard a recording of an investor call in December 2013. Holmes said: we have also been doing a lot of work for special operations command in the context of missions in remote areas.

Trial evidence showed that investors were told the technology was used to treat wounded soldiers and actually saved lives in the battlefield. The Department of Defense never used the Theranos analyser to clinically treat soldiers.

Theranos began working with drug companies in 2008 and 2009. A Pfizer representative testified that Theranos's answers to technical due diligence questions were oblique, deflective or evasive. After 2008, he said, Pfizer and Theranos had no meaningful business dealings.

Holmes admitted that she personally put the logos of Pfizer, GlaxoSmithKline and Schering Plough onto reports with favourable conclusions about the Theranos device. In emails to Walgreens she described the reports as the drug companies' independent and technical validation. Representatives of those companies testified that they never validated the technology and never authorised the logos. The reports were shared with investors.

Investors were also told that Theranos would grow from a few dozen Wellness Centers in Walgreens stores to 900. A Walgreens representative testified that the partnership was actually contracting.

◆Part 6: The Patients

Holmes and Balwani were also charged with defrauding patients. The indictment alleged that they paid for advertisements to persuade people to buy Theranos blood tests at Walgreens stores in California and Arizona.

A patient testified that an HIV test she took through Theranos came back positive. 2 later tests showed she was negative. Another patient testified that she received incorrect results from a Theranos test about her pregnancy.

The US Attorney's Office set out more examples from the trial and the sentencing papers. A physician repeatedly received extremely elevated PSA results from Theranos. Elevated PSA is an indicator of prostate cancer. Conventional tests proved the results inaccurate.

A pregnant woman who had suffered multiple miscarriages received HCG values that strongly indicated she was miscarrying again. Conventional tests showed a viable pregnancy. She went on to deliver a healthy baby.

A Theranos employee who took blood thinning medicine received results that differed markedly from earlier conventional tests. A doctor adjusted the medication. A traditional lab found the employee's levels were normal. The adjustment had been unnecessary.

The jury at Holmes's trial acquitted her of the patient conspiracy count and of 3 counts of fraud against individual patients. The jury at Balwani's trial convicted him on every patient count.

◆Part 7: The Inspection

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services oversees blood testing laboratories. In late 2015 it made an unannounced inspection of the Theranos lab in Newark. The onsite survey ended on 20 November 2015.

On 25 January 2016 the agency wrote to the lab director. The letter was headed Condition Level Deficiencies, Immediate Jeopardy. It found that the lab's deficient practices in haematology posed immediate jeopardy to patient health and safety. It listed 5 Conditions of certification the lab did not meet. It gave the lab 10 calendar days to respond.

The Statement of Deficiencies ran to 121 pages. It recorded that a quality control sample for a blood clotting test on a Siemens instrument was run 7 times on 7 September 2015 without an acceptable result. On 8 September it was run 12 times without an acceptable result. 81 patients were reported from 1 April to 16 September 2015.

For the Theranos devices, the lab's own validation plan required the coefficient of variation between replicate control results to be not more than 15 percent, or 20 percent at the limits of detection. On 1 Edison device, vitamin B12 controls from 5 to 30 January 2015 varied by 34.3 percent and 48.5 percent.

Holmes hired Dr Kingshuk Das in March 2016 to review and respond to the report. A patient impact assessment he worked on analysed the lab's 2014 and 2015 quality control data. It concluded that there was a possible patient impact for every test reported from the lab's Edison instruments. He found the Edison unsuitable for clinical use. He told Holmes that the instruments apparently were not performing from the very beginning. She attributed the results to laboratory control failures, not to the device.

Theranos voided every patient test result generated by the Edison. The Court of Appeals upheld the trial judge's view that this was an admission by Theranos, and by extension Holmes, that the results were, and always had been, unreliable. It also held that parts of Das's testimony were expert opinion that should have been tested as such. It found that error harmless.

Investors testified that in 2016 Holmes downplayed the inspection.

◆Part 8: The Charges

On 14 March 2018 the SEC charged Theranos, Holmes and Balwani with raising more than 700 million dollars from investors through an elaborate, years long fraud. Holmes settled without admitting or denying the allegations. She agreed to a 500,000 dollar penalty, a 10 year bar from serving as an officer or director of a public company, the return of 18.9 million shares and the loss of her voting control of Theranos. The SEC said it would litigate its claims against Balwani.

On 14 June 2018 a federal grand jury indicted Holmes and Balwani. The indictment returned on 28 July 2020 charged each with 2 counts of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and 10 counts of wire fraud. 1 conspiracy count concerned investors. The other concerned patients.

The trials were severed. Holmes was tried first, in late 2021. Her trial lasted nearly 4 months, with over 900 exhibits and testimony from 32 witnesses.

On 3 January 2022 the jury convicted Holmes of conspiracy to defraud investors and of 3 counts of wire fraud against individual investors. Those wire transfers totalled more than 140 million dollars. The jury could not reach a verdict on 3 other investor counts. 1 patient count had been dismissed at the prosecution's request because 1 patient would not testify.

On 7 July 2022 a separate jury convicted Balwani on all 12 counts.

◆Part 9: The Sentences

Judge Edward Davila found a loss of 120 million dollars and 10 or more victims. Holmes faced a guidelines range of 135 to 168 months. The judge refused the government's request for enhancements for reckless risk of death or serious bodily injury and for a leadership role. He refused Holmes a reduction for acceptance of responsibility. He imposed 135 months, the low end of the range, with no fine.

The US Attorney's Office summarised the government's sentencing memorandum. By the end of 2014 Holmes's stock in Theranos was valued at more than 4 billion dollars. She lived in a 15 million dollar mansion and travelled in a private jet paid for by Theranos.

On 7 December 2022 Balwani was sentenced to 155 months. Trial evidence showed he had owned nearly 30 million Theranos shares, over 6 percent of the company. The government's sentencing memorandum, as summarised by the US Attorney's Office, quoted his ambition: build the true American empire. A monopoly. Our obligation to USA.

Holmes began serving her sentence on 30 May 2023. On 29 December 2023 the Inspector General of the Department of Health and Human Services excluded her from Medicare, Medicaid and all federal health care programs for 90 years. The Departmental Appeals Board affirmed the exclusion on 16 January 2026.

On 24 February 2025 the Ninth Circuit affirmed both convictions, both sentences and the restitution order. It described the restitution as 397 million dollars for the investors identified at sentencing and 54.5 million dollars for Safeway and Walgreens. It held that the trial court should also have considered what the shares were still worth. It found the error harmless, because the trial court had found the investors were unable to sell their shares after the fraud came to light.

On 26 March 2026 Judge Davila cut Holmes's sentence to 123 months under a retroactive guidelines change for defendants with no criminal history points. The government opposed. It said she had not accepted responsibility, had paid minimal restitution and was advising her partner on raising money for a new company whose prototype diagnostic device, the government argued, bore striking similarities to the Theranos device. The judge found that no individual investor had been shown to suffer substantial financial hardship. He wrote that her notoriety will subject her to substantial scrutiny in all of her future endeavors.

On 5 October 2026 the Supreme Court of the United States denied Balwani's petition for a writ of certiorari. Justice Thomas took no part.

The guests watched the machine. The machine was running a null protocol. Some of their samples went to machines made by other companies.
